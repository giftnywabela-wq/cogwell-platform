Fanatical Prospecting — Cold Calling Mastery (Deep Dive Edition)

Key stats: 80% of sales require 5+ touches · 44% of reps quit after 1 follow-up · 92% of prospects say yes by touch 6 · 3× more likely to close with a referral.

The Mindset Reset

Everything starts here. Your pipeline is empty because your mind hasn’t accepted what this job actually demands. Until you do, no script, no training, no tool will save you.

“Fanatical prospectors are relentless. They prospect when they don’t feel like it, when it’s inconvenient, and when someone tells them to stop.” — Jeb Blount

The 3 Brutal Truths (internalize all 3 or nothing works)

Truth 1: An empty pipeline is the only real failure - Write on an index card: “My pipeline is my salary. I fill it every single day.” Say it out loud every morning before touching your phone. - The 30-Day Rule: what you skip today hollows out your results in 90 days. - Track your pipeline visually (whiteboard, kanban) so a thin pipeline feels uncomfortable enough to drive action. - Outcome: prospecting stops being optional — as non-negotiable as showing up.

Truth 2: Prospecting is interrupting — own it - You’re calling someone who didn’t ask for the call. Stop apologizing for it in tone or opener. - Ask yourself before dialing: “Do I genuinely believe my product/service can help this person?” Hesitation = a belief problem, not a prospecting problem. - Ban “just” from your vocabulary (“I’m just calling to…”). Replace with “I’m calling because…” - Stop saying “sorry to bother you” or “I know you’re busy” — these validate every reason to hang up.

Truth 3: Rejection is the job description - Set a daily rejection goal — literally aim for 20 “no’s” a day. Hitting 20 means you worked. - Rejection isn’t personal — they reject the interruption and the timing, not you. - After every rejection, write down the exact words used. This becomes your objection library. - Volume is the cure for rejection sensitivity — the best reps get rejected more because they dial more.

Build Your “Why” Anchor

  • 10-minute unfiltered writing exercise: “Why do I need to succeed in sales?” Narrow to one sentence and post it visibly.
  • Revisit and update every 90 days as your income and goals evolve.

Pre-Call State Ritual (5 minutes, every block)

  1. Minute 1–2: hype playlist, stand up.
  2. Minute 3: read your “why” aloud, then: “I am the best at this. I help people. Today I will make 50 dials.”
  3. Minute 4–5: read your script out loud, twice.
  4. Smile before you dial — it audibly changes your voice.
  5. Stand during calling blocks for more vocal authority and energy.

Eliminate “Getting Ready to Get Ready” (the silent pipeline killer)

Five avoidance behaviors disguised as work: - Research paralysis — cap research at 90 seconds per prospect (name, title, one company fact). - Script perfectionism — a draft today + 20 real calls tomorrow teaches more than 20 hours of editing. - CRM over-logging — during calling blocks, log only Attempted / Voicemail / Spoke / Outcome; details come in Platinum Hours. - Inbox management — check email every 2 hours during Golden Hours, not constantly. - Social media “research” — 90 seconds, get the one fact, get out.

Jeb’s Fundamental Laws

The 30-Day Rule — Today’s prospecting pays off in 90 days; skipping today creates an invisible gap 30–90 days out. Never skip a day because “the pipeline is full right now” — that’s exactly when most reps stop and create the slump they’ll later blame on the market. Mark a wall calendar every day you hit your dial number; never let a 2-day miss become 3.

The Law of Replacement — Every prospect that advances or exits must be immediately replaced. Calculate your monthly leak rate: (prospects removed or lost) ÷ (total prospects in pipeline); add new prospects faster than your closing ratio just to stay flat, more to grow. After every meeting/proposal, add 3 new prospects same day.

The Law of Need — The more desperately you need a sale, the less likely you are to get it; desperation is detectable (pace, follow-up frequency, price caving). The cure for desperation is more prospecting, not more pressure on the existing pipeline. Never call a prospect more than once per day across any channel.

The Law of Familiarity — The more familiar a prospect is with you before you call, the less friction you face. Before calling blocks (Platinum Hours), send LinkedIn connection requests with a brief personal note; send a priming email the morning before calling; reference these on the call itself.

The Three B’s — Be Brief. Be Bright. Be Gone. — Opener to ask under 30 seconds. Cut filler words (“um,” “so,” “basically,” “just wanted to check in”). After the ask, go silent for a full 5 seconds — the first person to speak after the ask is in the weaker position.

The Law of Consistency — Elite reps don’t have good/bad weeks of effort, only consistent ones. Build a non-negotiable daily dial floor and never go below it. Track a 30-day rolling average of dials as your true baseline. Example math: 40 dials/day × 22 days = 880 dials/month; at 8% connect = 70 conversations; at 25% appointment rate = 17–18 appointments.

Time Blocking — Your Daily Operating System

Golden Hours (e.g. 8–10am, 4–6pm): revenue-generating activities only — calls, conversations, meetings. Nothing else happens here.

Platinum Hours (before 7am, after 6pm): admin, research, CRM, email, LinkedIn outreach, list building — everything that supports selling but isn’t selling.

Ideal Daily Schedule

5:30–7:00am Build tomorrow’s list, research, CRM admin, email sequences, LinkedIn outreach Platinum
7:30–7:45am Pre-call ritual: music, “why,” script review, stand up Ritual

Protecting Golden Hours

  • Block calendars as “Revenue Block — Unavailable”; decline non-emergency meetings inside them.
  • Interruption response: “I’m in a calling block — I’ll catch you at 10.” No apology.
  • Phone on DND for all apps except the dialer during blocks.
  • Never check email during a Golden Hour.
  • Calculate your hourly value (e.g. target income ÷ 160 hours) and post it as a reminder of what a wasted minute costs.
  • The Sunday Night Protocol — 30 minutes confirming Monday’s call list, protected blocks, no morning meetings, clean CRM, so Monday starts dialing at 8am with zero decision fatigue.

The 5-Step Telephone Prospecting Framework

  1. Get attention (0–5 sec)
  2. Identify yourself (5–8 sec)
  3. The bridge (8–20 sec)
  4. The ask (20–25 sec)
  5. Handle the brush-off (25–45 sec, max twice)

Step 1 — Get Attention

Say: “Hi [Name]!” — warm, upbeat, slight upward inflection, not flat, not a question. - First name only — “Mr./Ms. [Surname]” telegraphs salesperson instantly. - Pause 1 full second after their name. - Avoid: “Hi, is this [Name]?” / “How are you today?” / “Do you have a minute?” - Stand up, shoulders back, slight smile before dialing — it’s audible.

Step 2 — Identify Yourself

Say: “This is [Your Name] with [Company Name].” — period, not a question, no tagline yet. - Slow down specifically on the company name. - Never say “I’m just [Name] from [Company].”

Step 3 — The Bridge (reason for calling, about them not you)

  • Pain-based: “I work with [type of companies] to help them [specific outcome]. We’ve found that [industry] companies typically face [specific pain] — and we’ve built something that addresses that directly.”
  • Referral/research: “[Mutual contact / I was looking at your company] — [one specific thing noticed]. I thought there might be a relevant conversation here.”
  • Social proof: “We’ve recently worked with [similar company] to [specific outcome]. Given what you’re doing at [their company], I thought this might be worth a conversation.”
  • Write 3 bridges, rotate them to find what resonates. 90 seconds of research max per prospect feeds the bridge. 1–2 sentences maximum — longer becomes a monologue.

Step 4 — The Ask

Say: “I’d love to set up a 20-minute call to [specific purpose]. Are you free Tuesday at 10am or would Wednesday at 2pm work better for you?” - Always two specific options, never open-ended “whenever works.” - Keep the commitment small (20 minutes) — extend later if the conversation is hot. - After asking, go completely silent for 5 full seconds. The first to speak after the ask is in the weaker position.

Step 5 — Handle the Brush-Off

  • “Not interested”: “I completely understand — most people I speak with say the same thing before we’ve had a chance to connect. All I’m asking for is 20 minutes. If it’s not a fit, I’ll tell you myself. Does [day] work?”
  • “Send me some info”: “Happy to. So I send you exactly the right thing — what’s most relevant: [option A] or [option B]? And let me grab a time to walk through it with you — does [day] or [day] work?”
  • “I’m too busy”: “I respect your time — that’s exactly why I keep this to 20 minutes. Is [day] better or [other day]?”
  • “We already have a vendor”: “Great — most of the companies I work with had someone too. They brought me in because [specific gap]. Worth 20 minutes to see if there’s something we could add?”
  • Never argue with the brush-off; acknowledge first, every time. Never say “but” after acknowledging — use “and” or a pause. You get exactly two asks per call, then thank them and end the call.

Voice & Tonality — The Hidden Close

“On the phone, your voice IS your handshake, your suit, your office, and your smile. Master it and you master the call.” — Jeb Blount

The 5 Dimensions of Voice

  • Pace — speak 20% slower than feels necessary; slow down further on your company name, their name, and the ask.
  • Volume & energy — project slightly more than normal conversation; stand up to increase both naturally.
  • Inflection — vary pitch; avoid upward inflection on statements (sounds uncertain); use slight upward inflection on genuine questions.
  • Strategic silence — 3–5 seconds after the ask; silence after an objection signals confidence, not confusion.
  • Warmth — smile before dialing (changes larynx shape and audible tone); genuine curiosity about the prospect changes how you sound; use their first name once near the start, once near the close.

Do’s and Don’ts

Do: smile before every dial, stand during calling blocks, pause 5 seconds after the ask, end statements with downward inflection, slow down on names/key phrases, use full sentences, breathe deeply before dialing, record and self-review every session.

Don’t: say “sorry to bother you,” say “just calling to…”, rush the company name, use upward inflection on statements, fill silence after the ask, use filler words, ask “do you have a minute?”, let rejection show in your voice on the next dial, match angry/dismissive energy, talk faster when nervous.

Voice Training Protocol

  • Record every session; review 3–5 calls per session for filler words, pace, inflection, silence discipline, and warmth.
  • Daily warm-up: record your script standing up and smiling at your intended pace, then dial.
  • Weekly partner drill: random objections, rep must acknowledge/redirect/re-ask within 30 seconds, timed.

Voicemail & Email — Multiplying Touch Points

Power Voicemail Formula (under 30 seconds always)

  • Pain-based: “Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Company]. I’m calling because we’ve been working with [relevant type of company] to [specific outcome], and I thought it might be worth a conversation. I’ll try you again [day], but if you’d prefer to reach me first, I’m at [number] — [repeat slowly]. Again, [Your Name], [Company]. Talk soon.”
  • Curiosity-based: “Hi [Name], [Your Name] with [Company]. I have some information I think would be genuinely relevant to what you’re working on at [their company]. I’ll call back [day] — or reach me at [number] — [number slowly]. Looking forward to it.”
  • Referral/trigger: “Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] from [Company]. [Mutual contact] suggested I reach out — [one sentence why]. I’ll try you again [day], reach me at [number] — [repeat slowly]. Thanks.”

7 rules: under 30 seconds always; state your number twice (normal pace, then slow); sound confident and busy, not hopeful; always name your callback day; never ask them to “call when you get a chance”; vary the message across touchpoints; leave voicemails at the end of the block, after live attempts.

Cold Email (5 sentences max, Platinum Hours only, never a replacement for calling)

  • Template: “[Name] — I work with [type of company] to [specific outcome]. Companies like [reference] typically see [specific result]. I’d like to explore whether it makes sense to do the same for [their company]. Are you available for a 20-minute call [day] or [day]? — [Your Name]”
  • “I called you” sequence: “[Name] — I tried you [yesterday/earlier this week]. I work with [type of company] on [specific pain] and thought you’d be worth talking to. [Specific result]. Worth 20 minutes? — [Your Name]”
  • Rules: 5 sentences max; specific subject lines beat generic ones; the email’s only job is to get a reply or warm the next call, never to pitch; always reference the last call attempt; follow up on every email with a call reference on the next dial.

Multi-Channel Prospecting Cadence

Most decision-makers need 5–8 touchpoints before engaging. The 14-day, 8-touch sequence per prospect:

Day Channel Notes
1 Call + voicemail Pain-based bridge, voicemail template A
2 Email Reference the call, 5 sentences, appointment ask
3 Call Reference the email, different bridge angle
5 LinkedIn Connection request with personal note, no pitch
7 Call + voicemail Outcome-based bridge, voicemail template B
9 Email Completely different angle — case study or insight
11 Call Final attempt this cycle; state it plainly if they pick up
14 Break-up “I’ll stop reaching out — clearly the timing isn’t right. If that changes, I’m here.”

Rules: every touch has a different angle — never repeat the same message; never reference failure (“I’ve been trying to reach you”) — frame each contact as fresh intent; after 8 touches with zero response, move to C-tier or remove; track every touch (date, channel, message, outcome) and optimize on 90 days of real data.

LinkedIn as a Prospecting Channel

  • Pre-call connection request the day before calling, with a brief personal note.
  • Message once connected, same day, referencing the upcoming call.
  • Engage with 1–2 posts before connecting with key prospects.
  • Post one relevant piece of content weekly so prospects who look you up see a credible profile.
  • LinkedIn is Platinum Hours work only — never during Golden Hours.

Objection Handling — The 5-Step Turnaround

“The number one reason salespeople fail to convert objections is that they respond before they understand. Listen first. Understand second. Respond third.” — Jeb Blount

  1. Pause & breathe — 2 full seconds.
  2. Acknowledge — “I completely understand” / “That makes a lot of sense” — never “but.”
  3. Clarify — “Help me understand — when you say [X], what specifically concerns you?”
  4. Respond — address the real objection with one strong point, not five weak ones.
  5. Ask again — “Given that, does it make sense to spend 20 minutes together?” Max two asks total.

Objection Scripts

  • “Not interested” → “I completely understand — most people feel the same before we’ve connected. All I’m asking for is 20 minutes. If it’s not a fit, I’ll tell you myself. Does [day] work?”
  • “Send me some information” → “Happy to. So I send the right thing — what’s most relevant: [A] or [B]? And let me grab a time to walk through it — does [day] or [day] work?”
  • “I’m too busy right now” → “I completely respect that — that’s exactly why I keep this to 20 minutes. Is [day] better or [other day]? I’ll make sure we’re out in 20.”
  • “We already have a vendor” → “That’s great — most companies I work with had someone too. They brought me in because [specific gap]. Worth 20 minutes to see if there’s something we could add?”
  • “We don’t have budget” → “Budget is always a real consideration. Is it a constraint right now, or that you haven’t seen enough to justify exploring it? I’m not asking you to spend anything — just 20 minutes to see if it’s relevant.”
  • “Call me back in 6 months” → “Absolutely. Is there something specific happening around then that makes it a better moment? I’ll put [date] in my calendar. Can I send something brief in the meantime?”
  • “I’m not the decision-maker” → “Thanks for telling me — who would be the right person to speak with, and would you be comfortable making a brief introduction?”
  • “I’ve never heard of your company” → “Completely understandable — we’re not everywhere yet. We work specifically with [niche] and have done [result] for companies like [reference]. That’s why I’m calling — 20 minutes might change that.”

Gatekeeper Mastery

Gatekeepers are professionals doing their job, not obstacles. Learn their names, be straightforward about your purpose, and treat them with respect — they talk to their executives constantly and remember how they were treated.

Scripts

  • “What’s this regarding?” → “I’m calling to speak with [Name] about [specific, business-relevant topic]. Is he/she available?”
  • “Are you selling something?” → “I work with [type of company] on [specific area]. I have some information I believe would be relevant to [name]. Is he/she available for a brief call?”
  • “He’s not available, can I take a message?” → “Yes, please tell him/her [Your Name] from [Company] called about [topic]. I’ll also try again [day]. What’s the best time to reach him/her directly?”
  • “He doesn’t take cold calls” → “I completely understand — I’ll keep it brief. This is specifically about [very specific topic]. Could you let him/her know I called? I’ll try again [day].”

Advanced Tactics

  • Call before 8am or after 5:30pm — executives often answer their own phones then.
  • Ask for a direct dial: “Is there a number I can reach [Name] on in the future?”
  • Try a different number if you suspect your calls are being screened.
  • Use LinkedIn as an additional channel to unreachable executives, not a replacement for calling.
  • Build rapport with the gatekeeper over repeated calls — consistent professionalism sometimes earns advocacy.

Sales EQ — Emotional Intelligence as Competitive Edge

  • Self-awareness — know your emotional triggers (fear of silence, over-explaining, defensiveness).
  • Self-management — when a prospect is rude, get calmer and slower, not defensive; non-complementary behavior disarms aggression.
  • Social awareness — read pace changes, tone drops, sighs, and short answers; stop pitching and ask what’s on their mind when you hear hesitation.
  • Relationship management — every call ends with a defined next step; remember and reference personal details next time.

Likeability

  • Smile before dialing; use their name once near the start, once near the close; ask one genuine question about their world before pitching; never talk over them; match their vocabulary; validate their experience genuinely.

Handling Rejection Without Compounding It

  • Rejection is neutral data about timing, not about you.
  • After a rejection streak, physically reset: stand, shake out hands, three deep breaths.
  • Never carry one call’s energy into the next — dial fresh every time.
  • Track your worst rejection streak so later ones feel manageable by comparison.
  • The antidote to a bad block is more dials, not fewer.

Pipeline Management — The Prospecting Pyramid

  • A-tier — highest probability, call first every day.
  • B-tier — possible, nurture 2–3× per week.
  • C-tier — long-term seed, weekly or less.

Building Your ICP and List

  • Define industry, company size, geography, decision-maker title, and typical pain points before list-building.
  • For each prospect, capture: full name, correct direct number, accurate title, company, and one specific fact to use in the bridge.
  • Keep 150+ prospects active; rebuild before dropping below 100 to avoid rushed, low-quality additions.
  • Sources: LinkedIn Sales Navigator, ZoomInfo, Apollo, company sites, industry directories, chamber lists, conference attendee lists, referrals.

Pipeline Hygiene

  • Remove stale prospects after 8 unanswered attempts across all channels over 3 weeks.
  • Move unresponsive prospects to a monthly nurture touch or remove entirely.
  • Re-score A/B/C tiers monthly based on real signals (funding, leadership change, growth).
  • Weekly 15-minute Friday hygiene pass: who advanced, who dropped, who needs replacing or re-tiering.

Know Your Numbers — The Dial Target Calculator

Typical benchmarks: 40–60 dials/day target · 8–15% connect rate · 20–30% conversation-to-appointment · 2–4 appointments/day · 40–60% appointment-to-opportunity rate · weekly review cadence.

Calculator inputs to work backwards from an income goal: monthly income goal → avg commission per deal → close rate on proposals → appointment-to-proposal rate → conversation-to-appointment rate → connect rate (dial to conversation) → yields dials/day, conversations/day, appointments/week, proposals/month, deals/month.

Your daily dial number is a floor, not a target — never go below it.

The Weekly Metric Review (Every Friday, 30 minutes)

  1. Pull the week’s numbers: dials, connects, conversations, appointments set, appointments kept, proposals sent.
  2. Calculate ratios (connects per dial, appointments per conversation, proposals per appointment) vs. prior weeks.
  3. Identify the single weakest ratio — focus there, not on all five at once.
  4. Diagnose by ratio: low connect rate → time-blocking or list-quality issue; low appointment rate → opener/bridge problem; low proposal rate → discovery quality issue.
  5. Set one specific improvement action, write it down, and check it the following Friday.
  6. Run this for 12 consecutive weeks to systematically fix every bottleneck.

Elite Habits — What Separates the Top 1%

Daily Non-Negotiables

  • Morning: read your “why” aloud, confirm the call list is ready, complete the pre-call ritual, block social notifications, stand before the first dial.
  • Calling blocks: A-tier first; never stop mid-block to look something up; log in 20 seconds max per call; stand if possible.
  • Between blocks: follow up on morning voicemails, log detailed notes, schedule confirmed callbacks; don’t check email reactively.
  • Evening (Platinum Hours): full CRM update, tomorrow’s call list built with one research fact per prospect, 3 wins written down, 30 minutes of sales education, review your “why” again.

Weekly & Monthly Rituals

  • Every Friday: review metrics and ratios; identify the weakest one and write a specific fix; add new objections heard this week to the library; 15-minute role-play with a peer or mentor.
  • Last day of the month: compare month-over-month ratios; purge and re-score the prospect list; revise the script with real-call data; celebrate one specific win by name.

The Reading Curriculum (12-Month Sequence)

  1. Months 1–2 — Fanatical Prospecting: read twice, highlight action items, implement as you go.
  2. Months 3–4 — Sales EQ: shift from activity to conversation quality.
  3. Months 5–6 — Objections: apply against your own real objection library by this point.
  4. Month 7+ — People Follow You: leadership thinking, even as an individual rep.
  5. Always — Sales Gravy Podcast: weekly, ongoing professional education.
