Sales Mastery Playbook

Applying Jeremy Miner, Alex Hormozi & Jeb Blount across the full sales cycle

1. The Sales Process & Its Outcomes

Every stage in the sales cycle has one outcome it must produce before the deal is allowed to move forward. This is the master reference for what "done" looks like at each stage, and which trainer's expertise is the primary lens for building skill there.

Stage Main Outcome Lead Trainer(s)
1. Prospecting Qualified lead list matching your ICP Jeb Blount
2. Qualification Confirmed need, budget, authority, timeline Blount + Miner
3. Initial Outreach / Connect First meeting booked Jeb Blount
4. Needs Discovery Clear picture of pain points and buying process Jeremy Miner
5. Presentation / Demo Solution tied credibly to stated problem Hormozi + Miner
6. Objection Handling Concerns surfaced and resolved Jeremy Miner
7. Proposal / Negotiation Mutually agreed terms, documented Alex Hormozi
8. Closing Signed agreement, committed start date Hormozi + Miner
9. Onboarding / Handoff Smooth transition to post-sale owner — (gap; none of the three)
10. Retention / Expansion Renewed or expanded business — (gap; none of the three)

2. Trainer Expertise Map

Jeb Blount — Prospecting, Qualification, Initial Outreach

Known for "Fanatical Prospecting." His expertise centers on filling the funnel consistently and the outreach/cold-calling stage — getting conversations started in the first place.

Jeremy Miner — Discovery, Objection Handling, Presentation

Built NEPQ (Neuro-Emotional Persuasion Questioning): a question-based, psychology-driven approach to uncovering pain points and defusing resistance without a traditional hard-sell pitch.

Alex Hormozi — Proposal/Negotiation, Closing, Offer Design

Known for $100M Offers and $100M Leads. His expertise is in what you're selling and how the deal is structured — building offers so compelling that closing becomes easier.

Caveat: all three have expanded beyond their signature area over time. This map reflects where each made their name, not a strict boundary on what they teach.

3. Stage-by-Stage Training Detail

Stage 1 — Prospecting & List Building

Lead trainer: Jeb Blount

Emphasis: volume and consistency over cleverness. The "30-Day Rule" — today's prospecting activity determines your pipeline a month out. Daily activity metrics (dials, emails, conversations) are the leading indicator, not results.

Training drill

  • Apply the Value Equation to your own prospecting effort — Dream Outcome is a full pipeline; Effort & Sacrifice is what discipline attacks directly. Reduce friction: same time block daily, pre-built lists, no decision fatigue.

  • Daily rep: 1 hour, same time, tracked activity count, logged. Run for 14 straight days minimum before judging results.

Stage 2 — Qualification

Lead trainer: Blount (framework) + Miner (questioning style)

Blount gives you the checklist (budget, authority, need, timeline); Miner pushes you to extract that information through questions rather than interrogating a checklist at the prospect.

Training drill

  • Take Miner's "situation questions" category and write 10 versions that surface budget/authority/timeline naturally, without sounding like a form.

  • Qualify 5 real or role-played leads using only questions, no statements, for the first 3 minutes.

Stage 3 — Initial Outreach / Booking the Meeting

Lead trainer: Jeb Blount

Emphasis: his five-step cold call framework (pattern interrupt → intro → reason for call → question → commitment) and handling opener-stage objections ("send me an email," "not interested") — distinct from mid-funnel objections.

Training drill

  • Script and drill the five steps until natural, not memorized-sounding.

  • Build a bank of 10 opener objection responses and rehearse out loud, ideally recorded.

Stage 4 — Needs Discovery

Lead trainer: Jeremy Miner (NEPQ)

Emphasis: problem-awareness before solution-awareness. Never pitch until the prospect has stated the problem and its consequences in their own words. Tonality — curious, not interrogative.

Training drill

  • Learn and sequence: situation → problem-awareness → solution-awareness → consequence → commitment questions.

  • Take 5 old sales calls and rewrite every statement made as a question instead.

  • Record yourself running the sequence live; review tonality specifically.

Stage 5 — Presentation / Demo

Lead trainer: Hormozi (what to present) + Miner (how to present it)

Hormozi: you're presenting the Value Equation, not features — every claim maps to Dream Outcome, Likelihood, Time Delay, or Effort reduction. Miner: let the prospect connect the dots via consequence questions before you show anything — presentation should feel like confirmation, not persuasion.

Training drill

  • Rewrite a presentation as answers to the four Value Equation variables only.

  • Insert one Miner-style consequence question before each major claim.

Stage 6 — Objection Handling

Lead trainer: Jeremy Miner

Emphasis: don't counter objections — reverse them. Ask a question that gets the prospect to explain the objection further, which usually reveals the real issue or lets them talk themselves past it.

Training drill

  • List your 10 most common objections; write a reversal question for each instead of a rebuttal.

  • Role-play: respond only with questions for the first 30 seconds when objections are thrown at you.

Stage 7 — Proposal / Offer & Negotiation

Lead trainer: Alex Hormozi

Emphasis: by this point the offer should do most of the work. Stacking (bonuses, guarantees, scarcity, urgency), and pricing framed against value delivered, not cost.

Training drill

  • Full offer stack for the deal in front of you: guarantee options, bonuses, real scarcity/urgency.

  • Name the offer using the formula: specific outcome + timeframe + mechanism.

Stage 8 — Closing

Lead trainer: Hormozi (offer-driven) with Miner's commitment questions as the mechanic

Hormozi: if you're relying on closing "technique," the offer is probably weak — fix the offer first. Miner: the actual close is just a commitment question the prospect has already emotionally agreed to earlier in the conversation.

Training drill

  • For any lost deal, audit whether the offer or the discovery was the actual weak point before blaming the close.

  • Practice ending with a single, direct commitment question — no re-pitching.

Stages 9–10 — Onboarding & Retention

Gap: no single trainer among these three owns this territory. All three are primarily front-of-funnel through close. If retention and expansion matter to your business, pull from a different source — none of these three built their reputation here.

4. Principles Behind the Techniques

Jeb Blount — Prospecting & Pipeline

Core principle: Activity precedes results, and the gap between them is why people quit.

  • The 30-Day Rule: pipeline is a lagging indicator — a quiet pipeline usually reflects prospecting inactivity three or four weeks ago, not "a bad month."

  • Resistance to prospecting is emotional, not logical: call reluctance kills more pipelines than bad technique. The fix is behavioral — remove the decision by building a fixed daily system.

  • Activity metrics over vanity metrics: track what you control (dials, emails, conversations), not just deals closed.

  • Sales is a numbers game only if the right activities are done consistently — volume without consistency doesn't compound.

Jeremy Miner — Discovery & Objection Handling (NEPQ)

Core principle: People don't trust what they're told; they trust what they conclude themselves.

  • Problem-awareness before solution-awareness: presenting a solution before the prospect articulates their own problem triggers skepticism and resistance.

  • Tonality signals intent before words do: a curious, non-performative tone bypasses the defensive reflex a "salesy" tone triggers.

  • Questions transfer ownership of the conclusion: a fact the prospect states themselves is unarguable in a way a claim from the seller never is.

  • Objections are usually under-explored, not final: asking the prospect to elaborate reveals the real concern underneath.

  • Traditional sales training creates resistance by design: pattern-interrupting the expected script is what actually lowers defenses.

Alex Hormozi — Offer Design, Closing, Scaling

Core principle: Value is engineered, not asserted — a strong enough offer makes selling almost unnecessary.

  • The Value Equation (Dream Outcome × Likelihood ÷ Time Delay × Effort): perceived value is a ratio — increasing it by reducing friction works as well as increasing the promised outcome.

  • Price is relative to value, not cost: buyers compare price to the value of the outcome, not to what it costs to deliver.

  • Guarantees, bonuses, and scarcity should target specific objections, not just pad perceived value.

  • Naming and specificity increase perceived likelihood: a vague promise is discounted automatically; a specific, falsifiable claim is trusted more even though it's a bigger claim.

  • Volume and reps compound faster than "getting it right the first time": publish/pitch, get signal, refine.

  • Documentation as a growth loop: writing down what you learn as you learn it forces clarity and builds a searchable library of your own reasoning.

How the three principles connect

These map to different failure points in the sales process, not competing philosophies:

  • Blount solves for volume — no pipeline, no opportunity for the other two skills to matter.

  • Miner solves for trust — even with pipeline, prospects resist being sold; his method removes the resistance mechanism itself.

  • Hormozi solves for value perception — even with trust established, a weak offer forces you to convince rather than let the prospect want it.

Using Hormozi's Value Equation to structure the training plan itself (Dream Outcome, Likelihood, Time Delay, Effort) is the same principle applied reflexively — treating your own skill acquisition as a product whose adoption you're maximizing, using his own logic on yourself.

6. Duplication Test

The bar for whether a skill is actually learned (per Hormozi's own standard) is whether you can apply it cold, without notes, and explain your reasoning out loud.

  • Find a real business or an unseen case study and build a full offer live, narrating your reasoning as you go.

  • Record yourself. If you can't explain why each stack element is there, you've memorized the shape, not the logic.

  • Repeat across at least 3 different business types (service, product, info/coaching) to force generalization rather than memorization of one example.
