NEPQ Discovery Question Sequence

Before you dive in
What this is
Let the prospect conclude for themselves that the problem is worth solving.
What it does
Situation, problem, solution, consequence and commitment questions per vertical.
Use it when
Inside the diagnostic call.
How it connects
Comes from: Sales Mastery Playbook

Deep dive

Jeremy Miner's Neuro-Emotional Persuasion Questioning, sequenced. Never pitch until the prospect has stated the problem and its consequences in their own words. Tone: curious, not interrogative.

The Sequence (generic)

  1. Situation-Awareness — establish the current state, no judgment.
  2. Problem-Awareness — let them name the problem themselves.
  3. Solution-Awareness — surface what they've already considered.
  4. Consequence — the real cost of leaving it as-is.
  5. Commitment — a question they've already emotionally agreed to.

Solar

  1. Situation: "How are you currently handling quote turnaround once a site visit's done?"
  2. Problem: "Where does that process tend to slow down or get stuck?"
  3. Solution: "Have you tried anything to speed that up before? What happened?"
  4. Consequence: "If leads keep going cold in that gap, what does that mean for the pipeline over the next 6 months?"
  5. Commitment: "Given what we've walked through, does it make sense to look at closing that gap?"

Home services

  1. Situation: "How are you currently handling calls that come in after hours or mid-job?"
  2. Problem: "Where does that usually break down — is it the answering, the quoting, or the follow-up?"
  3. Solution: "Have you looked at anything for this before — an answering service, a call system?"
  4. Consequence: "If this keeps happening, what's that doing to repeat business and referrals?"
  5. Commitment: "Does it make sense to look at how this could actually get fixed?"

Auto services

  1. Situation: "Walk me through what happens after an estimate goes out."
  2. Problem: "Where does follow-up usually slip — timing, who owns it, something else?"
  3. Solution: "Has anything been tried to tighten that up before?"
  4. Consequence: "If that gap stays as-is, what's it doing to monthly closed jobs?"
  5. Commitment: "Worth looking at how to close that gap?"

Med/dental/aesthetic clinics

  1. Situation: "How's rebooking and reminders handled right now?"
  2. Problem: "Where do no-shows tend to happen most — first visits, follow-ups, a particular day?"
  3. Solution: "Has anything been tried — reminder texts, calls, deposits?"
  4. Consequence: "If the no-show rate stays where it is, what does that do to the schedule and revenue over a quarter?"
  5. Commitment: "Does it make sense to look at closing that gap?"

Drill

  • Take 5 old sales calls (real or role-played) and rewrite every statement made in them as a question instead.
  • Record yourself running the full sequence live on a real or role-played call; review tonality specifically — curious, not performative.
  • Run the sequence in a vertical you're less comfortable with at least once a week — the questions above are starting points, not a script to recite verbatim.
