Tool · First touch

Cold Open Script

Before you dive in
What this is
Win the first touch with a cold, correctly tiered prospect.
What it does
Problem-Agitate-Book openers with two angles and a voicemail per vertical.
Use it when
First contact by call, voicemail or message.
How it connects
Comes from: Pipeline-to-Booked System

Deep dive

For the first touch to a cold, correctly-tiered prospect. Asks nothing beyond a short call.

Structure

  1. State their specific problem, in their own language.
  2. Agitate briefly — the real cost of not solving it.
  3. Offer the small first step — not the full pitch.

Solar

Primary angle — quote-speed gap: "Solar installers usually lose leads in the gap between the quote request and the quote actually going out — by the time it lands, they're already talking to someone else. I've got a quick breakdown of what that delay is likely costing — worth a look?"

Secondary angle — permitting/follow-up gap: "A lot of solar shops lose deals not on price, but in the weeks between the site visit and the permit coming through — the lead goes cold waiting. I run a quick check on where that gap is costing installers like you the most. Want the 10-minute version?"

Voicemail version: "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with Full Booking Machine. Quick reason for the call — I work with solar installers on the gap between a quote request and the quote going out, which is usually where deals are lost before price even comes up. I'll try you again, or feel free to call back at [number]."

Home services (plumbing/electrical/HVAC)

Primary angle — missed calls: "Contractors like [Name] usually lose the job if they can't quote same-day — most callers just book whoever answers first. That's often 3–5 jobs a month walking to someone else. I ran a quick missed-call/quote-speed check on businesses like yours — want the 10-minute version?"

Secondary angle — after-hours calls: "Most home-service businesses lose the highest-value jobs — the emergency ones — because nobody answers after hours. I checked what that's likely costing in after-hours bookings alone. Worth a quick look?"

Voicemail version: "Hi [Name], [Your Name] here with Full Booking Machine. I work with plumbing and HVAC businesses on missed-call recovery — most lose 3 to 5 jobs a month to whoever answers first. I'll try again, or call me back at [number]."

Auto services

Primary angle — rebooking: "Shops like [Name] usually lose repeat business to whoever calls back first after a missed call. I checked what that's probably costing in rebookings — want the quick version?"

Secondary angle — quote-to-close gap: "A lot of shops lose the job between the estimate and the customer actually saying yes — by the time you follow up, they've gone somewhere faster. I ran a quick check on what that gap is costing similar shops. Worth a look?"

Voicemail version: "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with Full Booking Machine. Quick reason for the call — most shops lose more to slow follow-up after the estimate than to price. I'll try you again, or call back at [number]."

Med/dental/aesthetic clinics

Primary angle — no-shows: "Clinics like [Name] usually lose the most revenue to no-shows, not to empty demand. I ran a quick look at what a no-show costs at your visit value — worth 10 minutes?"

Secondary angle — rebooking after treatment: "A lot of clinics do fine on new patients but leak revenue on rebooking existing ones for the next visit. I checked what that's likely costing at your visit value. Want the quick version?"

Voicemail version: "Hi [Name], [Your Name] with Full Booking Machine. I work with clinics on the cost of no-shows and missed rebookings — usually the biggest leak, bigger than new-patient marketing. I'll try again, or call back at [number]."

Notes

  • Swap in the real estimated cost once you have it from the diagnostic — specific numbers land harder than vague ones.
  • If they push back before the ask, don't defend the offer — ask a question instead ("What's your quoting process look like right now?") and let the Reply → Proof → Ask script take over.
  • Rotate between the primary and secondary angle by vertical when the first angle goes flat twice in the Pipeline Tracker — see the Content Idea Tracker for the validation discipline.
