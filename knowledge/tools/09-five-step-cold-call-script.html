5-Step Cold Call Script and Opener Objections

Before you dive in
What this is
Run a cold call in 45 seconds and survive the first brush-off.
What it does
The five steps, reason-for-call lines per vertical, ten opener objections with responses.
Use it when
Live cold calling.
How it connects
Comes from: Sales Mastery Playbook and Jeb Blount

Deep dive

Jeb Blount's five-step cold call framework, plus responses to opener-stage objections — distinct from mid-funnel objections, these happen in the first 10 seconds.

The 5 Steps

  1. Pattern Interrupt — break the expected "salesperson" cadence. "Hey [Name], I know this is a cold call — I'll be quick."
  2. Intro — who you are, plainly. "This is [Your Name] with Full Booking Machine."
  3. Reason for the Call — specific to their vertical, not generic.
  4. Question — hand the conversation to them.
  5. Commitment — a specific next step, not vague. "Worth 10 minutes this week to see if it's worth solving?"

Step 3 & 4, by vertical

Solar 3. "Reason I'm calling — I work with solar installers on the gap between a quote request and the quote actually going out." 4. "Is that something that's ever cost you a signed deal — a lead going cold before the number even lands?"

Home services 3. "Reason I'm calling — I work with plumbing, electrical, and HVAC businesses on cutting down missed calls and slow quotes." 4. "Is that something that's ever cost you a job — someone booking with whoever called back first?"

Auto services 3. "Reason I'm calling — I work with auto shops on tightening follow-up after an estimate goes out." 4. "Is that something that's ever cost you a job — a customer going quiet after the quote?"

Med/dental/aesthetic clinics 3. "Reason I'm calling — I work with clinics on cutting down no-shows and rebooking gaps." 4. "Is that something that's actually costing you — empty chair time from no-shows?"

Opener Objection Bank (first 10 seconds)

Objection Response
"I'm not interested." "Totally fair, you don't know what it is yet — 15 seconds: [reason for call]. If it's not relevant, I'll let you go."
"Send me an email." "Happy to — quick one first: is [missed calls / slow quotes / no-shows] actually something you deal with, or should I not bother?"
"We already have someone for that." "Good to know — most of the businesses we work with did too. What's that costing you outside of hours?"
"How did you get this number?" "[Honest source]. Is now a bad time, or can I take 20 seconds?"
"We're not looking right now." "Understood — can I ask what would need to change for it to be worth a look?"
"I don't have time." "Totally get it — can I grab 10 seconds and you tell me if it's even relevant?"
"Just send info." "I will — one thing first so I send the right thing: is [pain point] something you're actually dealing with?"
"We tried something like this before." "What happened with that one?"
"How much does it cost?" "Depends what you need — mind if I ask a couple questions first so I'm not guessing?"
"Who is this again?" "[Name] with Full Booking Machine — 15 seconds on why I called?"

Vertical nuance on objections

  • Solar — "we already have someone for that" usually means a CRM, not a competitor agency; the follow-up should probe the quoting gap specifically, not assume a competing vendor.
  • Home services — "just send info" is more common here than in other verticals; don't over-invest in beating it, move to voicemail/text follow-up instead.
  • Auto — "how much does it cost" comes up earlier than in other verticals; hold the line and qualify before pricing.
  • Clinics — "we're not looking right now" often means budget cycle timing (start of quarter/year); the follow-up question about timing is especially worth asking here.

Drill

  • Script and drill the five steps out loud until they sound like you, not memorized.
  • Rehearse all 10 opener responses out loud, ideally recorded, until none of them sound defensive.
  • Drill step 3 & 4 in at least 2 verticals per session so the reason-for-call line doesn't default to whichever vertical you know best.
