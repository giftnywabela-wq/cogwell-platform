Tool · Warm reply

Reply, Proof, Ask Script

Before you dive in
What this is
Convert a reply into a booked call.
What it does
Reply in their language, give one proof point, ask for a specific slot, with an example per vertical.
Use it when
As soon as a prospect replies or engages.
How it connects
Comes from: Pipeline-to-Booked System

Deep dive

For once a prospect has replied or engaged — a warm touch, not a cold open.

Structure

  1. Reply fast, in their language. Mirror the words they used, don't reset to a generic tone.
  2. Give one piece of real proof. A stat, a mini case, or the finding from their own diagnostic — not a generic feature claim.
  3. Ask for the call, sized to the trust already built. A specific time, not "let me know when works."

Template

"Good question — [restate their concern in their words]. On the [X] businesses we've run this for, the average was [specific stat]. Want to jump on a quick call [specific day/time] and I'll walk you through what it'd look like for [Prospect] specifically?"

Solar

Prospect: "We already qualify leads pretty well, not sure this changes much."

Reply: "Makes sense — qualifying isn't usually the issue for solar shops, it's the gap between the quote request and the quote landing in their inbox. On the installers we've worked with, cutting that gap from days to hours added 2–3 extra signed jobs a month. Want to jump on a 15-minute call Wednesday and I'll show you where that gap is costing you specifically?"

Home services

Prospect: "How is this different from just hiring someone to answer the phone?"

Reply: "Fair question — a person can't quote 24/7 or catch the calls that come in at 9pm when a pipe bursts. On the plumbing and HVAC businesses we've set this up for, same-day quote turnaround alone added 2–3 extra booked jobs a week. Want to jump on a 15-minute call Thursday and I'll show you what that'd look like against your current call volume?"

Auto services

Prospect: "We follow up on estimates already."

Reply: "Good to hear — the piece that usually gets missed is same-day follow-up, not follow-up eventually. On the shops we've worked with, tightening that window added 2–4 extra closed estimates a week without touching pricing. Want a 15-minute call Tuesday to see where the gap is for you?"

Med/dental/aesthetic clinics

Prospect: "We already send appointment reminders."

Reply: "Good — reminders help, but most no-shows still happen because there's no easy way for the patient to reschedule in the moment. On the clinics we've worked with, adding that alone cut no-shows by roughly a third. Want a quick 15-minute call this week and I'll walk through what that could mean at your visit value?"

Notes

  • One clear proof point, not three — piling on stats reads as convincing, not confirming.
  • The ask should be a specific slot, not an open-ended "let me know" — specificity is what actually gets booked.
  • Match the proof stat to the vertical's actual lever: solar = quote-speed, home services = call-answer speed, auto = follow-up window, clinics = rescheduling friction.
